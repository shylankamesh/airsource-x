HOW THE STUDY WORKS

From monitor
to measured test.

The forecasting pipeline creates separate 0–24h and 24–48h targets, then evaluates them on a chronological holdout.

  1. 01

    Aggregate & check

    15-minute rows become hourly values. An hour requires at least three valid PM2.5 readings.

  2. 02

    Build issue-time features

    Lagged pollution and calendar features are used; future observed weather is excluded.

  3. 03

    Compare on held-out issues

    LightGBM is compared with persistence and a training-only seasonal mean. Validation chooses the model; the later test period reports its performance.

EVIDENCE, WITH BOUNDARIES

What this result
can—and cannot—say.

We make uncertainty visible so the interface doesn’t turn a limited observation into an unsupported claim.

✓

Supported by this run

  • Historical PM2.5 forecast errors at ITO for the reported split.
  • Comparison with persistence and seasonal baselines.
  • Observed coverage, gaps, and model bias in the test period.
—

Not established here

  • Reliable as-issued or live forecasts.
  • Accuracy at other Delhi monitors, states, or cities.
  • Which source caused pollution, or any source percentage.
!
Forecast intervals under-cover.

The nominal 90% intervals covered 71.5% of 0–24h test cases and 74.6% of 24–48h cases. Treat them as exploratory, not calibrated confidence bounds.

DATA PROVENANCE

One station.
Clear provenance.

Source: OpenCity’s listing of CPCB ITO 15-minute observations. The downloaded file contains 68,985 records and explicit UTC offsets. In this analysis, timestamps are converted to IST before hourly aggregation.

The filename and listing identify a 2024–25 resource, but records extend to 1 January 2026. The file ends with one annual footer row that has no timestamp. It was excluded during hourly aggregation. Confirm the resource format and reuse terms with the provider before redistribution.
